# Egypt: library discovery and catalog feasibility

Research checked 15 November 2026 UTC. **Three institutions returned actual specific-book results: one substantial selected target and two smaller reserves. The requested ten substantial successes were not reached; the selected shortfall is nine.** The evidence retains 27 blocked or unverified entries, including multiple EULC origins rather than pretending they are separate institutions. No product integration, authentication, borrowing transaction or outreach was performed.

**Recommendation:** investigate an authorized, reliable **EULC/Future** interface for national academic reach; use **Koha** for an initial narrowly scoped Cairo University Faculty of Law candidate; keep **Misr Public Library** high on the public-lending list once its service and automated-access position can be verified. Numerous large Koha catalogs explicitly exclude automated searching. A working Koha parser alone does not unlock them.

## Market and common systems

Egypt has several overlapping library sectors. Dar al-Kutub is a national preservation/research institution. Bibliotheca Alexandrina (BA) has a large public-facing main library and specialized collections. Misr Public Library (MPL) is especially relevant to ordinary readers because it has an actual membership and external-lending model across a public network. Public universities share a substantial national discovery infrastructure, while private universities and international research institutes operate other catalogs.

The Supreme Council of Universities (SCU) identifies its Digital Libraries Unit, founded in 2007, as EULC's umbrella. It explicitly describes the **Future System**, developed with Mansoura CITC, and distinguishes its unified university-library catalog from thesis repositories, journal publishing and journal indexing. A connector must query physical-book holdings and preserve institution attribution; finding a thesis or an institution in a selector is insufficient. This is the strongest primary evidence for a high-reach common family, without establishing a national LMS market-share percentage. [SCU](https://scu.eg/en/eksc_units/digital-libraries-unit/)

**Koha** has repeated institution-specific evidence: official BUE and GUC documentation names it; MPL has published a Koha reporting guide; Cairo Law, NVIC and Universities of Canada returned recognizable live Koha search/record responses. IFAO names a Koha administrator and its catalog robots file exposes standard Koha routes. These named deployments support adapter reuse, not a census of Egyptian libraries. [BUE](https://lib.bue.edu.eg/online-tutorials/library-catalogue/), [GUC](https://guc.edu.eg/en/facilities_services/library.aspx), [MPL](https://mpl.org.eg/en/a-guide-to-koha-system-reports-at-the-egypt-public-library/), [IFAO](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/bibliotheque/bib-presentation/)

Current migrations matter. AUC announces **WorldShare Management Services from 14 January 2026**, so old IBIS/Sierra assumptions should not drive implementation. BA's older documentation names Virtua, while the current official `/catalog` link leads to a customized `balis.bibalex.org` ASP.NET OPAC. IDEO explicitly names its **Diamond-ILS** software, and the Institute of National Planning (INP) has a separate **VuFind** discovery site as well as a Koha catalog. None of these families was integrated here. [AUC](https://library.aucegypt.edu/auc-libraries-and-learning-technologies), [BA historical platform documentation](https://www.bibalex.org/libraries/presentation/static/12440.aspx), [IDEO](https://ideo-cairo.org/en/catalogue-en/alkindi-en/), [INP](https://discovery.inp.edu.eg/)

The Egyptian Knowledge Bank, DAR digitization, e-book subscriptions and journal platforms are useful services but are not substitutes for copy-level physical-book evidence. NRIAG also links an **Egyptian Libraries Network** union at `egyptlib.net.eg`; its overlap with EULC and its current platform remain unresolved. [NRIAG](https://www.nriag.sci.eg/egyptian-libraries-network/)

## Reader access and integration implications

An anonymous search does not establish admission or borrowing rights. The selected Cairo Law book is reference-only; general-public entry eligibility was not established. NVIC is particularly clear: free entry, no membership, and **no books may be borrowed**, even though its catalog says Available and displays a hold button. Universities of Canada describes borrower categories inside its community; outside-reader eligibility is unresolved.

MPL is the strongest broad public-lending opportunity found. Membership begins at age three; its homepage advertises up to five books for 14 days. Its membership page says non-Egyptians without at least one year of residence do not receive external borrowing. BA offers daily tickets or memberships, with age restrictions on the main reading area; its FAQ describes a researcher-only trial lending service from January 2023 requiring annual membership, two books for two weeks, renewable once. IFAO access is free for its eligible research audience; IDEO and CEDEJ materials are consulted on site. ARCE reports scholarly-user categories and currently announces a September–November relocation closure with limited appointments. These policies must accompany any future discovery integration. [MPL](https://mpl.org.eg/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B6%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%A9/subscription-system/), [BA](https://www.bibalex.org/en/page/faq), [IFAO](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/bibliotheque/bib-presentation/), [IDEO](https://ideo-cairo.org/en/library-en/), [CEDEJ](https://cedej.fr/catalog/), [ARCE](https://arce.org/library/)

For Koha, reuse is only a candidate. Tests would need Arabic Unicode handling; single-result redirects; edition IDs and ISBN retention; actual holding-library labels; reference-only institution overrides; and explicit treatment of Not for loan. Cairo Law also combines electronic carrier metadata with physical Books holdings, so a format label must not erase the actual copy evidence. No parser or live app checks were run in this research-only task.
