# Iraq: public physical-book catalog feasibility

Research date: 15 November 2026 UTC. **Seven institutions have actual book-search evidence: five selected targets and two reserves. The selected-ten goal is short by five.** Fourteen substantial priorities remain visible below; access failures were not replaced with small libraries merely to reach ten.

Start with **Baghdad’s Koha catalog**, then narrow adapters for the **National Library OPAC** and **Mustansiriyah’s physical-loans catalog**. The strongest demonstrated network opportunity is **lib.iq’s public union frontend**: one response contained actual items belonging to four identifiable institutions, and a second query specifically filtered Imam Hussain. This frontend requires its own JSON adapter and careful institution/edition handling. Later HTTP503 responses limit confidence in reliability. No app integration was attempted.

## Marketplace and platform evidence

The [IFLA Iraq profile](https://librarymap.ifla.org/countries/iraq), updated August 2025, describes a mixed structure: public libraries are managed locally under general Ministry of Culture supervision; university libraries are administered by university presidencies under the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research. Public/research libraries attached to shrines also supply collections, training and shared services. Its service-point table reports 1,836 overall (2021), with university/public/school categories dated 2019; its newer administrative-unit table is a different metric. These figures must not be presented as a current national OPAC coverage denominator. [Saved source](evidence/ifla-country.html).

**Koha is the best evidenced shared implementation target, not a measured national majority.** Baghdad’s live OPAC, Mosul’s unified central/branch homepage, and Hamdaniya’s live search identify Koha. [Mosul’s October 2026 report](https://uomosul.edu.iq/en/libcentral/2026/10/13/the-director-of-mosul-university-libraries-holds-a-meeting-with-the-librarians-of-the-branch-librarians-to-follow-up-on-the-development-of-work-and-the-digital-transformation/) describes ongoing bibliographic updates across its branches. [Basrah’s 2026 announcement](https://en.uobasrah.edu.iq/news/14353) describes adoption with hosting provided by Imam Hussain Shrine; that announcement does not establish a working public catalog. [lib.iq’s about page](https://lib.iq/about) names MARC21, Koha and Z39.50; its observed browser frontend uses Vue and a public JSON search route, so the existing Koha HTML adapter is not directly sufficient.

Legacy systems coexist. [Tikrit’s automated-systems division](https://en.tulib.tu.edu.iq/index.php/university-council/division-of-automated-systems) describes WinISIS maintenance, and the [National Library’s 2023 achievement report](https://www.iraqnla.gov.iq/nwe%20site/Achievements/pdf/2023/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D8%AC%D8%B2%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AB%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%202023.pdf) is indexed with WinISIS data-entry evidence. Neither proves the backend of the National Library’s public PHP OPAC. Indexed [Salahaddin eLibrary](https://elibrary.su.edu.krd/) pages name **SLiMS**, but access was challenged and physical holdings were not established. Mustansiriyah’s verified `/search` and `/view/loan` application is custom, although its official website separately advertises Koha.

A [2023 Iraqi journal issue](https://iasj.mohesr.gov.iq/journals/journal/issue/12755) abstracts a study of 100 shared book records across three participant catalogs, reporting duplicates and cataloging inconsistencies. Its narrow sample does not establish nationwide rates, but the current probe independently encountered a concrete edition-provenance problem: union record `80316` includes an IHL 1999 publication note and Al-Zahraa 1993 notes under a common description. Preserve local item provenance; do not carry a union ISBN/year indiscriminately to every institution.

The IFLA-described Iraqi Electronic Union Library/Arab Union Catalog initiative, Al-Abbas Iraqi Knowledge Portal, Iraqi author authority file, and IQDR thesis repository are distinct projects. Their names or institutional participation do not establish a common endpoint or physical holdings. IQDR’s 135,000-thesis narrative is not a book-catalog success. Likewise, Salahaddin’s electronic resources and Kufa’s explicitly campus-only DSpace service are not substitutes for physical-book searches.

Its JSON can still include other institutions’ items after a branch-filtered search, so filter each item by the requested institution. No internal or authenticated endpoint was used.

## Policies, failures and remaining limits

[Saved path-specific policy decisions](evidence/policy-decisions.json) use the shared longest-match evaluator. `lib.iq` allows its public search API and explicitly prohibits `/cgi-bin/`; those prohibited paths were not fetched. Baghdad robots 404 supplies no published rule and no reuse licence. National robots permits the OPAC paths. Mustansiriyah publishes an empty wildcard Disallow. Mosul’s policy comments express no applicable search prohibition. Hamdaniya distinguishes training-bot restrictions from public search; its English query returned zero and its Arabic `نهج البلاغة` query returned 503, so neither is counted.

Kufa University’s wildcard Disallow `/` remains in effect despite a separate crawl-delay group. Salahaddin and Technology challenge responses were not bypassed. Al-Kafeel robots timed out. Sulaimani’s official search instructions name `c-library.univsul.edu.iq`; that origin was blocked by the connection tunnel. Duhok’s robots 302 error route was not mistaken for a plain404. Officially linked Basrah and AUIS catalog origins, Al-Alamain’s candidate, and several later source requests returned 503. After the union’s later503, no more union requests were made. Root diagnostics independently found identical proxy503 responses on example.com, World Bank, NOUN and CERN during the late failure wave. Consequently the late503s are execution-network failures, not evidence of Iraq-specific outages. They never establish that a library does not hold a book.

[Sulaimani’s official instructions](evidence/sulaimani-search-route.html) explicitly exclude periodicals, theses and several language collections from its OPAC, and require a university membership card/ID for a library card. This matters even after access becomes possible. AUIS describes account services for students and staff; anonymous book access was not reached. The other successful public searches do not establish visitor entry, borrowing eligibility or reuse permission.

Five selected successes are justified by national/university roles or dated substantial physical collections; two additional successes remain reserves because their collection scale was not established. No reconstruction/donation total is presented as current indexed holdings. English title evidence was obtained; the bounded Arabic attempt failed and Kurdish querying remains untested. There were no product edits, authentication, holds, outreach, private-IP requests, challenge/TLS bypasses or persistent browser/session state.
