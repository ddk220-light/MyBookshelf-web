# Uganda: physical-book catalog expansion research

**6 institutions returned observed book records: 4 selected targets and 2 reserves. The ten-substantial-library target remains short by 6 selected institutions.** Twenty major priorities remain visible below, including inaccessible national/public and larger university collections. Research completed 15 November 2026; no application integration, account use or outreach.

The strongest immediate route is Koha reuse for **Gulu, MUBS and KIU**, with a separate custom frontend for **Bugema**. Koha is directly evidenced in these catalogs, not inferred from neighboring countries. The CUUL/RENU VuFind interface could offer broad discovery reach, but its two actual searches returned zero results. Its six member labels cannot establish usable indexed holdings or six successes.

## Marketplace and access

Uganda combines national legal-deposit and public-library coordination, municipal public libraries, multi-campus academic services and specialized legal/research collections. The National Library of Uganda describes support and book distribution to more than 50 public/community libraries; its over-30,000-books statement is distribution activity, not current collection size. Makerere also serves a national-reference/legal-deposit role. CUUL organizes collaborative research-information access, including subscriptions and partner services; e-journal access, repositories and ebook discovery do not establish physical copies. Sources: [NLU](https://nlu.go.ug/library-backgrund), [Makerere](https://mulib.mak.ac.ug/about-us/), [CUUL](https://cuul.or.ug/).

Koha is the best-supported reusable family in this feasibility sample: five native OPACs returned books, and Bugema’s public frontend links its records to Koha. [Muni’s history](https://lib.muni.ac.ug/library-history/) dates its implementation to 2016; [Kyambogo](https://lib.kyu.ac.ug/online-resources/) explicitly identifies Koha; Makerere’s [2023 CoBAMS annual report](https://news.mak.ac.ug/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Makerere-CoBAMS-Annual-Report-2023.pdf) describes Virtua. These are institutional adoption observations with different dates, not national market shares. CUUL/RENU itself displays VuFind, a discovery layer that is distinct from member circulation systems.

Public catalog access does not establish reader entry or loans. [NLU rules](https://nlu.go.ug/rules-and-regulations) require a paid entry pass and say books are used in the reading room. [KCCA](https://www.kcca.go.ug/library/library) publishes daily/monthly/annual membership fees; its catalog could not be queried. [KIU](https://library.kiu.ac.ug/services/borrowing-and-circulation) requires borrower eligibility and identification. MUBS requires registration and allows applications from external users under published conditions; its dated 2016 access description must not be treated as current pricing. [LDC](https://www.ldc.ac.ug/ldc-library/) describes a closed-access legal collection with subscriptions for external users. No membership was purchased and no loan was initiated.

Gulu’s one-hit search redirected to record 602, so the query’s total count is unknown rather than a fabricated count. MUBS displayed 27 records; KIU and Ibanda each displayed 3, including related books; Bugema displayed 2, including an unrelated title. The selected book in each case has its own exact identifier. MMU’s “Research methods” is explicitly a **related-title** query: 15 matches included the fully recorded *Research Methods: Concepts, Process and Practice*. MMU and Ibanda stay reserves because substantial collection scale was not established. Bugema is a selected regional university target based on its explicit physical-title/copy claims and five-campus service; those numbers are site claims, not an audit.

Bugema’s successful route is the normal public GET form at `lib.bugemauniv.ac.ug/physical-books/`, which publishes `Allow: /`. The distinct `bulibrary.bugemauniv.ac.ug` Koha origin explicitly excludes native search, and that search route was not requested. Its returned record link provides the observed Koha identity 5983. A permitted custom frontend adapter is the candidate, not an assumption that native Koha search can be used.

## Network and adapter implications

- **Koha:** reuse the existing app adapter only after offline replay and operator-policy review. This research did not run application tests. Gulu requires support for search-to-detail redirects; MUBS mixes exact and related editions; KIU’s hard-copy notes and location fields must survive parsing.
- **Restricted material:** Gulu explicitly separates Available, Not For Loan and In transit. MMU’s Available items remain in Reserved Books Section. These are distinct facts and should not be collapsed into unrestricted borrowing.
- **Bugema:** custom HTML search exposes title, author, Koha ID, call number, shelf section, campus, barcode and copy status. Query count includes false-positive results; identity must use the actual returned record, not the count.
- **CUUL/RENU:** the landing page names Makerere, Kyambogo, Gulu, Mbarara, Busitema and UMI and declares VuFind/RENU. `Things fall apart` (Title) and `research` (AllFields) both returned zero. No institution-filtered holding was proven. Before integration, verify a functional index, filters, provenance, update cadence and whether live availability comes from member catalogs. The existing VuFind integration is deployment-specific.
- **National/public libraries:** NLU is a natural coordination channel, but its governance and distribution role do not prove one interoperable catalog across the public network. No NLU branches or KCCA constituent libraries were counted independently.

## Evidence and limits

[findings.json](findings.json) contains the common schema, exact query metadata, selected/reserve status, returned book details, scope/size qualifications, reader-access notes and integration issues. [evidence/policy-decisions.json](evidence/policy-decisions.json) records longest-match robots decisions for successful search routes. The `evidence/` directory contains bounded raw HTML bodies and request metadata, including blocked robots responses, failed searches and source pages. Files ending `discovery.json` contain source-index evidence and are not substitutes for successful catalog requests. Unrelated indexed public login text and signed asset links were removed from discovery output.

No national largest-ten ranking, market-share percentage, reuse licence or live-app integration is established. Two selected libraries rely on explicitly stated facility/institutional scale proxies. A robots 404 means no published rule was found, not affirmative permission. Robot failures stopped that origin. Zero-result/challenge/network responses never establish that a title is absent. Availability is a timestamped catalog observation, not a guarantee at arrival. Catalog branch labels, consortium membership, repositories and subscribed ebook inventories are not independent physical-library successes.

No product files were changed. No additional library was promoted merely to fill the target.
