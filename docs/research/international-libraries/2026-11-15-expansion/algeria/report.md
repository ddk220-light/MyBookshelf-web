# Algeria: international-library opportunity screen

Research date: **15 November 2026 UTC**. **10 selected physical-library institutions returned actual book-search results:** five through local PMB catalogs, one through local SYNGEB, and four through institution-filtered CCdz queries. **One additional reserve** is the National Library's digital manuscript catalog. That reserve does not establish physical circulation coverage. No national largest-ten ranking is claimed.

The strongest observed entry point is the university sector. Build a scoped **CCdz adapter** for breadth and an **Arabic/French PMB adapter** for richer local copy information; add **SYNGEB** for local collections such as Béjaïa. CCdz gives institution-associated bibliographic listings in this evidence, while PMB and local SYNGEB can expose available and restricted physical copies. National and wilaya public libraries remain important priorities; their general physical catalogs were not verified here.

[Machine-readable findings](findings.json) contain the common schema, exact methods, request payloads, UTC timestamps, source URLs, policy decisions, title identities and holdings. [Evidence](evidence/) contains bounded responses and request metadata. This was research only: no accounts, holds, outreach, application edits or deployment.

## Marketplace, access and shared-service leverage

Algeria has national heritage collections, a Ministry of Culture public-library structure at wilaya level, and substantial university central/faculty library networks. The Ministry's indexed descriptions distinguish the National Library's preservation responsibilities from public reading libraries. The official Jijel description lists **more than 37,000 titles and 127,000 copies university-wide**, but without a reliable current census date; it is a useful scale signal, not a national ranking. The Chlef public-library news report concerns **more than 42,000 titles digitized in February 2026**, a different unit from physical volumes. Chlef's university and public library are different institutions; only the university was verified.

CERIST's [SYNGEB product description](https://www.dist.cerist.dz/site-syngeb/presentation.php) documents acquisitions, cataloging, internal/external and interlibrary loans, public and collective catalogs, ISBD/UNIMARC, and French/Arabic interfaces ([raw source](evidence/cerist-home.html)). Live forms at Béjaïa and UMMTO identify themselves as **OPAC Syngeb**. Béjaïa returned physical holdings; UMMTO's actual query timed out. This establishes important deployment evidence, not a national market-share estimate.

PMB is also a material family. Mostaganem's [official deployment page](https://www.univ-mosta.dz/fst/catalogue-en-ligne/) says its PMB catalog network has operated since **2018** ([raw source](evidence/mosta-platform.html)). Five independent universities returned PMB book results in this screen. Feasibility selection, accessible servers and the available title choices strongly affect that sample. It does not establish PMB's share of all Algerian libraries.

[CCdz](https://www.ccdz.cerist.dz/) is a national collective catalog operated by CERIST. Its homepage says it identifies/localizes documents rather than supplying full text. The advanced form supports a book-type filter and institution location identifier. Four actual result responses repeat an explicit central-library name and return book rows:

These are actual filtered title-search results, not membership entries or selector counts. The whole national union is **not** counted as an extra library. Record **145450** occurs in both the Constantine and Annaba responses; two independently scoped returned searches establish the separate institutional associations. One unscoped record would not suffice. Public detail pages expose title, author, edition and MARC identity, but the general localization action redirected to a missing authentication page. No workaround was attempted. Copy counts, loan status and entitlement remain unknown for these four institutions.

[SNDL's official description](https://www.dist.cerist.dz/sndl.php) distinguishes subscribed international electronic resources available within campuses from research resources requiring an individual account, and from national services including CCdz, PNST and journal portals ([raw source](evidence/cerist-sndl.html)). **SNDL subscription access, PNST theses, institutional repositories and IQRAA ebooks cannot substitute for physical-book holdings.** A public title result also does not establish visitor entry or borrowing. USTO's [current rules](https://www.univ-usto.dz/buc/?page_id=500) reserve access to registered students/teachers and expressly authorized outsiders; borrowing needs a reader card, with reference and on-site materials excluded ([raw rules](evidence/usto-access.html)).

## Policy, implementation and remaining blockers

Robots policies were saved before catalog searches. CCdz explicitly allows `/`; Béjaïa's wildcard exclusions do not cover the observed `/opac/` routes. Chlef, Mostaganem, Batna 2, USTO and Biskra returned robots **404**: no published rule was found, which is **not a reuse licence**. Named restrictions and longest matching paths were checked with the shared helper. Ghardaïa's policy permits search/reference use and prohibits training; its zero-result probes were not elevated into successes. No blocked robots origin was repeatedly queried for its local catalog; successful CCdz routes are independent public origins with their own retained policy.

PMB needs deployment-specific work. This is not a claim that MyBookshelf's existing pinned PMB implementation accepts all five deployments unchanged. Batna 2 requires a fresh anonymous selection of `database=Biblio_Centrale`. USTO and Saïda use public CSRF form fields; anonymous cookies were held only in memory. Some PMB searches first return counts by field, requiring the exposed title-results POST. [Session recipe](session_query.py) and per-query chain metadata preserve that sequence. Captured nonces are transient, not durable identifiers. No reader login, reservation, purchase suggestion or other write action was submitted.

Mostaganem's results expose the database string **`pmbdb_utf8_test`** in an RSS link. The institution's actual central-library records, copy labels and official PMB deployment description support selection; a database name alone does not establish a demo. The exact central deployment's production freshness remains unconfirmed. Unquoted `Le petit prince` produced 869 broad token matches; the quoted phrase produced zero. Neither is claimed as evidence for that book. The distinctive quoted title in the selected table returned one actual matching record.

The blocked alternatives are substantive: Alger 1 showed maintenance; two scoped CCdz title probes returned zero. UMMTO and Relizane title queries timed out. ENP yielded an incomplete response; a bounded prefix had no usable search form, so no success was claimed. Tlemcen, Sétif 1, Batna 1 and Jijel had robots transport failures. Ghardaïa's two Arabic title attempts and Saïda's two featured-title attempts returned zero. Zero results never establish that a library does not hold a book. [Full blocker ledger](findings.json) retains each failed check and its evidence path.

**Transport limitation:** root-level controls independently diagnosed a broad execution-network outage around **08:21 UTC**. The late Biskra detail, Guelma CCdz query and Mostaganem linked-faculty fetch returned identical immediate upstream HTTP 503 errors. These are execution-transport observations, **not evidence of Algerian institution downtime**. Earlier title successes remain valid observations. No new live probes were made after the transport diagnosis.

No aggregate national stock survey, current market-share estimate, general public-library access audit, live application adapter test, or production integration was performed. The selected ten are substantial institution priorities with observed title responses, including four union-only bibliographic associations. Copy restrictions and unknown circulation must remain visible in any future product.
