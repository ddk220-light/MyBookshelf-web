# Kenya library expansion research

Research date: **15 November 2026 UTC**. **6 institutions returned book records: 5 selected targets and 1 smaller reserve. Four selected targets returned positive physical holdings; Strathmore returned a book record explicitly showing zero physical items.** The target of ten substantial verified institutions was not reached. Eighteen major priorities and 32 blocked or unverified candidates/routes remain visible in [findings.json](findings.json). This is a feasibility shortlist, not a national largest-ten ranking.

The strongest near-term opportunity is a bounded Koha pilot with **University of Eastern Africa, Baraton; Africa International University; Daystar; and Scott Christian University**. Their actual search and detail responses expose physical locations and copy statuses. Strathmore needs its physical holdings coverage explained before it can support availability results. Tom Mboya is a useful smaller reserve. All six are research observations, not completed MyBookshelf integrations.

Kenya combines large university systems, a national bibliographic institution and county/community public libraries. **KNLS WebCat is the potentially largest shared-catalogue opportunity**, but its operational endpoint, participants and institution-specific holdings were not verified. Its April 2025 government launch report describes a pilot addressing fragmented institutional catalogues; it does not prove national live coverage. Current KNLS information identifies Maktaba Kuu and two branches, Buruburu and Nakuru. Historical 64/66-library figures must not be presented as the current KNLS network. [WebCat government announcement](https://gaa.go.ke/knls-launches-national-union-catalogue-dubbed-webcat-initiative-maktaba-kuu), [current KNLS service scope](https://knls.ecitizen.go.ke/).

[KLISC](https://icolc.net/participating-consortia/kenya-libraries-and-information-services-consortium) is a useful cross-sector coordination route for resource sharing, electronic licensing and training. Its consortium membership does not establish one LMS or a usable physical-book union endpoint. [Book Bunk](https://bookbunk.org/collections/) offers a particularly relevant public-reader opportunity through McMillan Memorial, Eastlands and Kaloleni: its 2019 inventory of 137,705 books, 16,000-plus Koha titles uploaded, and digitized archival objects are different measures. No operational public title-query route was located.

**Koha is the best evidenced common adapter family.** All six successful catalogues expose Koha generator metadata and search/detail routes; [Kenyatta's official library description](https://library.ku.ac.ke/about-library/) and [Egerton's 2025 staff training](https://www.egerton.ac.ke/eu/library-latest-news-updates/university-library-staff-trained-on-koha-integrated-library-system-to-enhance-services-and-operational-efficiency) add institution-specific adoption evidence. A [2013 university OPAC study](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/EJC166152?download=true) reported Koha in close to half its online-OPAC sample; that historical scoped sample and this feasibility-selected set are not current national market-share estimates. Older V-smart/Iguana, SirsiDynix, Liberty, ABCD and AMLIB references require deployment-specific confirmation. Egerton's older AMLIB page and newer Koha training illustrate why documentation alone cannot establish the currently deployed catalogue.

## Access and implementation implications

UEAB robots allows the tested search/detail routes while excluding exports. The five other successful catalogue hosts returned robots 404: absence of published rules is not an affirmative reuse licence. No authentication was used. [Daystar](https://www.daystar.ac.ke/daystar-library/library-access) requires university registration for facilities and borrowing. UEAB's handbook allows approved external reading-only cards without checkout. [Scott](https://library.scott.ac.ke/library-services/) allows authorized external readers without borrowing; AIU describes external research users but complete borrowing terms were not established. Public catalogue access alone does not establish visitor entry or borrowing eligibility.

The existing Koha adapter is a **reuse candidate**. Before product integration, validate saved fixtures against its parser, resolve canonical hosts, preserve institution/campus and home/current locations, and keep item type and shelving restrictions. Available textbooks in a Processing Center should not automatically become generally borrowable copies. Preserve lost, checked-out and in-transit states, rather than flattening them into presence or availability. Strathmore's zero-holdings record must never become a physical availability hit. UEAB's observed search/detail times of approximately 18/22 seconds require realistic timeout budgets. ISBNs and edition-specific record IDs should remain attached to individual records.

## Blocked alternatives and limits

Policy exclusions prevented searches at **JKUAT, KMTC, Kenya Methodist, Karatina and USIU-Africa**. More-specific search exclusions override a root Allow rule. Karatina's permitted details do not authorize its prohibited search route. **St. Paul's and Zetech** returned HTTP 200 browser-challenge shells, not book results; no bypass was attempted. Pan Africa Christian's robots response itself contained a browser check, so even its HTTP 404 did not provide a basis to query.

Robots checks failed with 503/timeouts at major and alternative candidates including **KNLS, Nairobi, Kenyatta, Moi, Pwani, Kabarak, Embu, CUEA, KCA, DeKUT, Meru, Machakos, Laikipia, Technical University of Mombasa, KEMRI and Amref**. These are dated environment observations, not claims of permanent public outage. Mount Kenya's zero-result searches are not evidence that the books are absent. Egerton, Maseno and Book Bunk lacked a verified current query route. MMUST's template-like library counters were rejected as collection-size evidence. Daystar's functional independent OPAC was counted once; its failed older hostname and unverified VuFind institution facets did not create extra institutions.

The machine-readable dossier preserves the specific URL and evidence for every blocked route. Responses were bounded, with normal TLS verification and robots checked before catalogue requests. No private-address catalogue was probed; no credentials, holds, outreach, challenge bypass or product changes occurred. All findings are a single-session snapshot. Size sources have different dates and formats, and no national largest-ten ranking, national LMS share, full-network holdings completeness or live MyBookshelf adapter test has been established.
