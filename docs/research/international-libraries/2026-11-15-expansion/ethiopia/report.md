# Ethiopia — library catalog expansion research

**2026-11-15 UTC: 3 observed successful institutions, 3 selected, 0 reserves; 7 short of the requested ten selected.** The successful catalogs are Abrehot Library, the Admas University library network, and the African Union Commission Library in Addis Ababa. Thirteen substantial priorities remain visible below, including all ten initial national/public/university priorities. No national largest-ten ranking is established.

**Recommendation:** pursue Koha reuse through the academic-library community while treating Abrehot and the AU Library as separate custom integrations. CEARL is the strongest evidenced coordination opportunity. The public endpoints verified here do not demonstrate a national union catalog, national LMS market shares, or ready-to-release integrations.

## Market and network leverage

Ethiopia combines large university networks, a national heritage institution, public reading libraries, NGO-supported children's libraries and international research collections. These are different reader markets: a public catalog does not make a university or headquarters library freely borrowable. The AU Library, for example, describes institutional-letter and photo-ID requirements for student/researcher cards; anonymous catalog searching is independently available. [AU access policy](https://library.au.int/faq).

The Consortium of Ethiopian Academic and Research Libraries (CEARL) offers the best institutional reach. EIFL's current country page says it works with **64 institutions** through the consortium. ICOLC describes collection sharing, electronic licensing, interlibrary loan, training and automation expertise. The linked 2024 membership document is an older snapshot, not evidence that all current members share one technical system. A coordinated inventory of official Koha endpoints could unlock considerably more than configuring a single successful campus. No contact or outreach was undertaken. [EIFL Ethiopia](https://eifl.net/country/ethiopia), [CEARL functions](https://icolc.net/participating-consortia/consortium-ethiopian-academic-and-research-libraries).

Koha is the best-supported reusable family in this investigation: it returned live books at Admas, Hawassa explicitly names it, and Arba Minch describes an installed Koha system. A November 2018 university-hosted study covers Koha at Addis Ababa, Debre Berhan and Wolkite, using convenience sampling. This is corroborating institutional evidence, not a national market-share survey. A separate October 2015 study documents **ABCD** at Jimma and Mekelle; that historical evidence must not be silently converted into a current Koha claim. [Hawassa](https://www.hu.edu.et/library), [Arba Minch](https://amu.edu.et/en/library-and-information-service), [2018 study](https://repository.ju.edu.et/handle/123456789/4562), [2015 study](https://repository.ju.edu.et/handle/123456789/4555?show=full).

Live/public evidence also shows a custom Laravel/Livewire catalog at Abrehot, a Drupal 7 catalog interface at the AU Library, Soutron at the National Archives and Library portal, and a current Primo link for UNECA. The underlying ILS at Abrehot and AU remains unknown. These require separate implementation decisions, not a generic “Ethiopia” adapter.

For public reach, Ethiopia Reads reports establishing over 80 libraries across all regions. It is an important network prospect, but no shared physical-book OPAC was identified. The National Academic OER & Digital Library, university DSpace repositories, Ethiopian Journals Online and electronic subscriptions concern digital discovery, scholarship or licensed content; they cannot establish physical-book holdings. [Ethiopia Reads](https://www.ethiopiareads.org/projects/libraries/), [national OER platform](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/).

## Blocked alternatives and limits

Additional meaningful candidates were investigated: Ambo University and St. Mary's University catalog robots timed out; Dire Dawa returned a routing 403 and its 2025 user manual describes campus-network access; EGST's indexed Koha catalog returned proxy 503 for robots on both independently indexed hosts. Arba Minch documents Koha but a current public physical-catalog endpoint was not established. Adwa Victory Memorial had an allowed, functioning interface, but its catalog showed zero titles and an actual `The Battle of Adwa` query also returned zero. It is **not** a book-search success or reserve. [Full blocker inventory](findings.json).

NALA's licence-error page was not bypassed or modified. UNECA's official current link leads to the `41UNOG_INST:ECA` Primo view, whose robots explicitly disallows `/discovery/search` and `/primaws`; neither was requested. Gondar's private-address catalog was not probed. No credentials, persisted cookies, challenge workarounds, disabled TLS, holds, purchases, outreach or product changes were used.

Proxy 503, routing 403 and timeouts describe this research environment's result. They do not establish a countrywide outage, absence of a book, or inaccessibility to local readers. Most scale sources are undated, and indexed official assertions are labeled as such. Successful public searches do not establish permission to reuse data, visitor admission, borrowing eligibility or live circulation. These limitations explain the **three selected / seven shortfall** outcome; the list was not padded with repositories, branch selectors or unverified search snippets.
