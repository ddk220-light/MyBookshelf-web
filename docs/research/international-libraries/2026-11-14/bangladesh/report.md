# Bangladesh library expansion: ten verified academic targets

**Result:** ten recommended institutions and one reserve returned real book records and item holdings through public catalog searches on **15 November 2026 UTC**. All 11 use Koha. These are a feasible integration shortlist, **not a defensible claim to be the country’s ten largest libraries**: several substantially larger institutions have inaccessible or robots-restricted catalogs. No app provider was configured, enabled or deployed.

## Marketplace and what unlocks the most

Koha offers the strongest demonstrated adapter reuse. Every successful catalog here uses its standard public CGI search/detail pattern. The existing MyBookshelf generic Koha parser passed offline replay for the ten targets; CUET and RUET failed the richer title parser and need parser work for that route. This observed, feasibility-selected sample does not establish national LMS market share. The evidence supports “best first platform,” not a fabricated national percentage.

The public-library opportunity is distinct from the ready academic catalogs. A [March 2026 study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-026-01315-1) surveyed 59 professionals from 48 of 71 government public libraries and found substantial reliance on manual workflows and infrastructure gaps. Its percentages should not be treated as a deployment census: respondents and institutions are different denominators. The government also already offers [Amar Library](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.misfit.publiclibrary), updated August 2025, claiming centralized government catalog search, physical-book availability, membership and interlibrary transfers. Its official site, http://elibrary.dpl.gov.bd/, timed out from this environment; no API or actual book query was verified. This is the strongest potential public-reader network partnership to investigate, while independent Koha catalogs are the proven near-term route. Existing initiatives mean the opportunity is complementary discovery/interoperability, not an assumption that no national digital service exists.

The live [BANSDOC National Union Catalog](https://nucb.bansdoc.gov.bd/) displayed 113 participating libraries, 5,373 journals and 9,563 holdings. It is a **journal** union catalog. Those 113 participants must not be counted as immediately unlockable book libraries. UGC Digital Library/LiCoB subscription e-resource arrangements likewise do not prove public physical-book federation. CUET’s shared catalog exposes central/hall/seminar libraries; City exposes two campuses and Jagannath Central/CSE. Count these as branches within institutions, not extra independent targets.

All ten returned a real detail record after their title query. Single-request timings ranged from a few seconds to 22.83 seconds for Khulna detail. These are observations, not latency benchmarks. Search counts include keyword matches and editions, not necessarily exact-title unique books. Availability is a point-in-time holdings observation. Reference-only, damaged and checked-out copies must retain their meanings; a visible record is not proof of borrowability.

No integration licence or API agreement was established. A 404 robots response means no rule was published at that path, not affirmative reuse permission. Requests used a descriptive research user agent, ordinary unauthenticated GETs and normal TLS verification. Four recommended targets use officially linked HTTP origins (CUET, RUET, City, IUT); actual onboarding must review those exact origins. KUET’s reachable `library-backup` host needs canonical-host/freshness confirmation. Chittagong explicitly documents multiple catalog replicas with different update schedules.

## Follow-up scale evidence

The follow-up established official book counts for KUET (>50,000), CUET (>52,000, indexed official page) and RUET (nearly 40,000, August 2025). For Jagannath, Prothom Alo reports 31,326 central-library books in October 2025; this is explicitly secondary evidence. Its official service page reports 24,000 e-books and a historical Liberation War subcollection, neither an official physical-total confirmation. [Collection updates](collection-updates.json) record each source, unit and limitation.

## Recommended sequence

1. Start institutional onboarding with Chittagong, Khulna and IUB: live HTTPS catalogs, primary scale evidence and existing-parser reuse. Chittagong contributes the largest verified collection; Khulna needs latency handling and correct reference-only display.
2. Add IUT, City, KUET, Jagannath, Sylhet and the two engineering universities after their specific origin, freshness, parser and membership issues are resolved. This broadens city/discipline coverage but does not create open public borrowing rights.
3. Explore DPL/Amar Library for public-reader reach and a supported network interface. Revisit larger blocked targets through appropriate institutional interfaces/partnerships; do not bypass their robots or authentication restrictions.

## Evidence and reproducibility

The machine-readable [findings.json](findings.json) contains per-query UTC timestamps, exact GET URLs, HTTP status, elapsed time, returned book/record ID, detail requests, holdings, scale sources, robots evidence and limitations. `evidence/*-search.html`, `*-detail.html`, headers and metadata are saved raw snapshots for offline parser replay. [probe.py](probe.py) and the numbered input batches document requests. The sibling [adapter replay summary](../adapter-tools/koha-replay-summary.json) records compatibility tests. Research date differs from the inherited batch folder date: all direct probes here ran 15 November 2026.

Unused exploratory artifacts are not positive evidence: `ruet-detail-candidate` was an unrelated guessed candidate and is excluded from findings; `adust-detail` is CD/DVD 34855, excluded in favor of `adust-detail-book`33012. This report relies on the latter verified result records.

All statuses are point-in-time. Tests did not log in, place holds, borrow books, send messages, contact libraries or enable application integrations.
