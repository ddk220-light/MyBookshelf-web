Weekly Centralian Link (April 6, 2018)

Henry Luce III Library: A labor of love


Henry Luce III, President of the Luce Foundation and his step-son together with past CPU President, Dr. Agustin A. Pulido during the inauguration of the Henry Luce III Library.

Albert Einstein, the famous physicist, once said, “The only thing that you absolutely have to know is the location of the library.”

This line holds true not only for bookworms but for academicians who understand that the pursuit of knowledge is found in the rigorous devotion to reading.

Established in 1991, the Henry Luce III Library is one of the largest libraries in the Philippines—the biggest in Western Visayas. It is home to more than 250,000 volumes which includes 40,000 United Nation documents. It also hosts the American Studies and Resource Center, Meyer-Asian Collection and the Elizabeth, Knox Music Collection and notable artworks of Centralian high school alumna Ms. Nelfa Querubin. The university is also a recipient of the Thomas Jefferson Information Center in Manila and the United States Information Service.

Presently, one will find the CPU College of Law Office, the BSP Knowledge Resource Network, the World Bank-Knowledge Development Center, the Transnational Office, Office of International Relations and Cooperation and the American Corner at the Henry Luce III Library.

The library is currently under the leadership of Dr. Melda L. Estember, CPU Director of Libraries.

Standing tall and proud, the Henry Luce III Library stands witness to the thousands of Centralian scholars who persevered the long hours of study and research until they have reached professional success.

Previously the university library was found in the Lopez Hall, but along the years as the university grew, so was the need to accommodate more books and reading materials to the new breed of Centralian scholars.

The Henry Luce III Library is a labor of love of CPU’s longest-serving university president, Dr. Agustin A. Pulido. The journey of building a one of a kind library is a story of faith and unity—a joint effort of the entire Centralian community. “We had this idea that the next time we had an opportunity-we should give priority to a library.”

CPU was assisted by the United Board, they nominated the university to the American Schools and Hospitals Abroad (ASHA), a federal program sponsored by the U.S. Government. “We indicated to the United Board that our priority at that time was a library. Of course we already had a library at that time—it was the Lopez Hall—but it was very inadequate by that time.”

CPU submitted a project to the United Board for a new library building estimated to cost $960,000. “The university was very fortunate because United Board would normally recommend only one school from an Asian country at a time. We were excited. Later, they said CPU made it to the first screening.”

However during the second screening, CPU lost its bid but United Board continued to help the university. They recommended CPU to the Luce Foundation which eventually sponsored three-fourths of the fund. “United Board continued to help us. Not long after that, the Luce Foundation, which was a good friend of the United Board, was celebrating their anniversary. They wanted to make a donation to a university and asked the United Board for recommendations—the United Board recommended CPU.”

Finally, the United Board wrote a letter to CPU saying that they would support the project. The agreement was that Luce Foundation would sponsor one-half of the $960,000. A big campaign was launched by Engr. Aurora Homena Alerta-Lim. “A year before the deadline, at some point, we thought that we couldn’t really raise the $480,000. We wrote to United Board and said that this didn’t seem workable and if they could talk with the Luce Foundation and ask if they were willing to increase their share. The United Board and the Luce Foundation were so generous that our share was reduced to $240,000. We had to raise it by the end of the year, but we accomplished it.”

With the funds ready, the university invited three architects to present their design. Sisi Castro, a well-known architect who had projects in different parts of Asia, and two others from Iloilo submitted their ideas. Castro’s design was chosen with the revision that instead of making two buildings, the design would be converted to a three-storey building.

Henry Luce III Library was finally inaugurated. The CPU community gathered to celebrate a milestone in Centralian education. Henry Luce III himself and his stepson were present during the inauguration. In March 1997, Henry Luce III visited CPU again as the commencement speaker. In his message, he inspired Centralians to pursue success and generosity hand-in-hand.

In 2008, the university added an additional floor to the Henry Luce III Library. This holds the Transnational Office, the Office of International Relations and Cooperation, two conference rooms and one exhibit room holding the collections of records and memorabilia of past university presidents.

Fast forward 27 years later from its first opening, the Henry Luce III Library is a beloved façade in the CPU Campus, holding true to CPU’s commitment of producing exemplary Christian learners for life.
