Why the phone view feels AI-made

The pieces are fine on their own. Together they follow a recognisable template: a slogan under every title, small caps labels on everything, every element in its own outlined box, and a costume on each screen. Natural apps say less, show the real thing (the covers), and use plain controls.

  1. 1A slogan under each title“Find your next read / Find a book, then see where you can read it.” “Make yourself at home.” Real apps name the place: Search, Settings.
  2. 2Small caps labels everywhereGOOD MORNING, YOUR LIBRARY, SEARCH IN, AT YOUR LIBRARY, PUBLISHED. Settings stacks “YOUR LIBRARY” twice. Use sentence case, or no label at all.
  3. 3Costume on every screenA wooden drawer around results, with a brass label repeating your query (“the hobbit / All books”). A thick brown frame around book pages, and stamps inside boxes. The decoration looks applied, not part of the product.
  4. 4One card, five screensThe green “YOUR LIBRARY” card repeats on Shelf, Search, Book, Libraries and Settings, each time followed by “Change library→”.
  5. 5Boxes inside boxesFields, outline buttons, segmented controls, cards and the page frame share one 12 px radius and border. Two full-width buttons stack under every field. On the book, the back link sits beside a large pill.
  6. 6Made-up spinesBooks appear as purple, olive and green bars in generated colours. Recently viewed looks like a bar chart, while real covers sit unused in the data.
  7. 7Explaining too much“Saved on this browser only…”, “These are suggestions to explore, not confirmed library holdings”, “Check the library's membership…”. Say each once, where it matters.
  8. 8Arrow links“Choose a library→”, “Find a book→”, “Change library→”: underlined, with the arrow jammed on, several per screen, all competing with the buttons.
  9. 9A box around the headingAfter every search, a green outline is drawn around “Search results”. It looks like a stray box, not a focus indicator.
  10. 10A floating tab barA floating pill with a filled dark blob for the active tab. Native tab bars sit flush, with a hairline and a tinted icon.
Rules for a natural phone view
  • Name the place, don't pitch it. Titles are nouns: Shelf, Search, Libraries, Settings.
  • Show real covers. Covers carry the warmth. Keep wood for the bookshelf only.
  • Use rows, not boxes. Hairline-separated rows, with one card style kept for the availability panel.
  • Say it once. Show the library as one tappable line. Put privacy notes in Settings.
  • Use plain controls. A filled search field with icons, a flush tab bar, system font for the interface, serif for books.
  • State status in words. A coloured dot and a sentence (“Similar edition in the catalog · 823 TOL”), not a drawn stamp.
