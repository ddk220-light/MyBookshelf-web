Privacy policy

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Your native app data stays with you. Your shelf, notes, ratings, and library activity are stored on your device. The native apps use no advertising, analytics, or tracking SDKs. You do not create an account with myBookshelf.

This policy covers the myBookshelf native apps for iPhone and Android (also known as MyBookshelf). Website privacy is described below. The apps are developed by Deepak Ravikumar.

Website privacy

You can find books, save a guest shelf, and check public library catalogs without an account. An optional private website account lets you keep your selected library and saved books across browsers. The website does not collect library-card numbers or PINs and cannot access your native app’s shelf, notes, ratings, checkouts, or holds.

Guest copies and private accounts

As a guest, your selected library, saved books (including free books and papers) and recently viewed books stay in this browser’s local storage. Appearance and companion preferences also stay on the device. Search terms are not saved in that storage. Clearing browser data removes guest copies. Creating or signing into an account does not automatically import those copies: you choose whether to bring books over, whether to include recent books, which library to use, and whether to remove unchanged imported copies from this browser after confirmation. Copies in other browsers remain separate. Free books and papers stay on the browser-only shelf and are not included in account imports.

For an account, we store your private name, verified contact email, sign-in methods, selected library, saved book references and their display details. This is a private account, not a public profile or social network. We do not sell reading data or use it for advertising. Account data is kept on the server and in the active tab’s memory; it is not copied into guest browser storage. Your chosen reading light and companion preferences remain device-specific.

Account recently viewed history is off until you turn it on. When on, it records explicit book opens confirmed by the server and keeps at most the newest 50 distinct references for 30 days. Pausing stops new entries; existing entries remain until you remove them or they expire. You can remove an entry or clear account history across devices. Choosing to import recent guest books is separate consent and does not turn on future history collection.

Signing in and security

You can use email and password, or a configured Google or Apple sign-in method. Google and Apple receive the identity sign-in requests you choose to make; we store their stable account identifiers and the name/email information needed for your account. Apple relay email addresses are supported. Connecting a provider is an explicit account-settings action; matching email addresses alone do not merge accounts. We do not request access to your contacts, messages or provider documents.

Passwords are stored as hashes, never as readable passwords. When screening a proposed password against known breaches, we send only the first five characters of a SHA-1 hash to Pwned Passwords, with padded responses. We do not send the password or full hash to that service. Verification, recovery and security notices are required service email; our configured mail provider processes the address and message for delivery.

Secure, HttpOnly account cookies keep you signed in. An Apple sign-in may use an additional short-lived cross-site correlation cookie. Sessions expire after seven days of inactivity and require a fresh login after 30 days absolutely. Sensitive changes require a fresh confirmation. You can inspect coarse device labels and sign out devices from account settings. Security and abuse controls process limited request/device information and keyed hashes of rate-limit subjects; they are not reading analytics. Provider access/refresh tokens retained for revocation are encrypted and never shown to the browser.

Export, deletion and retention

Account settings lets you download versioned JSON of your profile, preferences, sign-in method names, library, saved books and unexpired history after confirming your identity. The attachment excludes password hashes, credentials and session identifiers. We do not create a persistent download link. A file you download is under your control.

Deleting an account requires recent authentication and a separate explicit confirmation. It ends account access immediately and targets live private-data cleanup within 24 hours. During a storage outage, the status page distinguishes pending recording from durably accepted cleanup. A status receipt stays only in the submitting tab’s memory, so keep that tab open to check an uncertain response. Closing it does not stop a received request.

Supported database backups expire within 30 days. A restricted record of opaque internal identifiers is kept outside restored database snapshots for 35 days after live cleanup, so a restore cannot revive a deleted account. Restores must reapply these records and invalidate sessions before account traffic resumes. The minimum encrypted provider-revocation material may be retried for up to 30 days; delayed cleanup is monitored. Short-lived verification/reset challenges, recent-auth proofs and operation receipts expire and are purged. Queued token email expires with its link, and message ciphertext is removed after sending or expiry. A final deletion notice may be queued separately for up to 24 hours.

Deleting an account does not erase separate guest copies, another browser’s storage, your native apps, or files you downloaded. You can clear guest saved books from Settings and erase other local website data through your browser’s site-data controls.

Book lookups, camera, location and hosting

Free-book searches use imported public metadata. Paper searches are sent through the server to selected research indexes, such as Europe PMC, DOAJ, arXiv or OpenAlex. Temporary shared search and record caches reduce repeated requests and enforce source limits; they do not contain your saved shelf. Reading links open the original host, whose privacy policy applies there.

Web lookups send search terms, selected library identifiers and book identifiers to the website’s server for public catalog requests. Metadata and covers come from approved book/catalog providers, which process network requests under their own policies. We do not attach your account identity, private shelf or reading history to public metadata requests. Barcode recognition happens on your device; camera images are not uploaded.

If you choose Use my location, coordinates are sent to our server to find nearby libraries. They stay only in the open tab’s memory, not saved browser preferences or application telemetry. City and postcode searches go through our server to Photon (Komoot), using OpenStreetMap data. Opening a map loads OpenStreetMap tiles; that service receives your IP address and viewed map areas. You can search by library name or area without granting location access or opening a map.

Vercel hosts the website and private storage; the configured Postgres provider stores account data, and the configured mail provider delivers account messages. These services process operational network requests and may retain operational/security logs under their policies. The website runs no advertising or analytics scripts and does not log private shelf/history contents or authentication tokens in application telemetry. For privacy questions, export/deletion help or concerns, contact deepakdhanavel@gmail.com.

Books and personal library activity

Books you save, your reading status, private ratings, notes, and archived books are stored in the app’s local database. If you connect a supported library account, its checkouts, holds, due dates, and pickup status are retrieved directly from the library or its catalog provider and cached on your device. The developer does not receive this information.

Library card and PIN

Library sign-in is optional. A card number and PIN you enter are sent over HTTPS directly to your chosen library’s catalog service to authenticate and retrieve your library activity. They are never sent to a myBookshelf server.

On iPhone, credentials are stored in the device Keychain with device-only protection and are not synchronized through iCloud Keychain. On Android, credentials are encrypted using an Android Keystore-protected key. The app uses the library session only to provide the features you request.

Book lookups and external services

To find a book’s cover, description, publication details, or available reading preview, the app sends an ISBN or your title/author search to Google Books and Open Library. Open Library cover images may be delivered by the Internet Archive. To check borrowing availability, it sends book identifiers or search terms to your selected catalog service, which may use BiblioCommons, Calil, or Aspen Discovery.

Cover images load from the book and catalog providers. These services receive the network requests, including an IP address, and handle them under their own privacy policies. The app does not attach your library card, PIN, private notes, or private ratings to book-metadata requests.

Links to Goodreads, book previews, and library websites open the relevant website. Interactions there are governed by that website’s policies. myBookshelf is an independent app and is not operated by those services or by your library.

Camera

The camera is used to recognize book barcodes while you use the Scan screen. Barcode recognition happens on your device. Camera images are not uploaded or saved by the app. You can search by title, author, or ISBN without granting camera access.

Location and maps

If you choose to find libraries near you, the app requests location permission and a location fix. Your coordinate is used on the device to sort the bundled library directory. The app does not send that coordinate to the developer or library catalogs. You can choose a library by name without granting location access.

Maps use Apple Maps on iPhone and the device’s map service on Android. Map-service requests are handled by the relevant provider under its privacy policy.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the app schedules local reminders using due dates and pickup information available on your device. It does not register you with a developer-operated push-notification server. Library refreshes depend on connectivity and the background time the operating system allows.

Retention, backups, and deletion

Local data remains until you change or erase it. Archiving a book hides it from active shelves and lets you restore it later. Signing out removes the selected library’s stored credentials and its session.

To erase the native app’s saved books, notes, ratings, library connections, and reminders, use Settings → Erase everything. Use this before uninstalling if you want to ensure that Keychain credentials are removed too; Keychain items can remain after an iPhone app is uninstalled.

The app has no account-based cloud synchronization. Your operating system’s device backup may include local app data under your Apple or Google backup settings. Device-only iPhone Keychain credentials cannot be restored onto another device.

Tracking and children

The native apps contain no advertising or analytics SDKs and do not track you across apps or websites. The developer does not sell your data. The app is not directed specifically to children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from them.
